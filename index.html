The Concept

Mirrorwalker is a choice-driven text adventure where an AI narrator doesn't just tell the story — it reads you. Every decision you make is quietly scored against MBTI personality dimensions, and the world reshapes its dialogue, tone, and endings around the person it thinks you are.

The game is built as three interconnected levels, each borrowing the DNA of a famous story to interrogate one facet of the self: conformity, self-identity, and the courage to break established boundaries.

I conceived the idea and designed the gameplay structure, themes, and story content; the choice-based MBTI system linking decisions to personality dimensions is also my design. My teammates handled most of the Dify-based technical implementation.

Three Worlds, Three Questions

Each level traps the player inside a story they think they know — then turns the mirror.

I

The Devotion of Suspect X

THEME · CONFORMITY

A perfect crime, a perfect alibi, a society that rewards those who disappear into their roles. Do you protect the person you love by becoming exactly what the world expects — even when the cost is yourself?

“How much of yourself would you erase to keep someone else whole?”

II

Cyberpunk 2077

THEME · SELF-IDENTITY

Night City, where memory is editable and chrome replaces flesh. When your thoughts can be copied and your body swapped, what is left that is unambiguously you?

“If your memories were rewritten tonight, would you still be you tomorrow?”

III

The Truman Show

THEME · BREAKING BOUNDARIES

A comfortable world built to keep you inside it. Every exit is labelled dangerous, every rule is "for your own good". The final level asks the only question that matters.

“The door was never locked. Will you walk through it?”

The MBTI Choice Engine

No quizzes, no self-report. Your personality is inferred from what you actually do under pressure.

Every choice quietly shifts one or more axes. The AI narrator reads the running profile and adapts dialogue, NPC attitudes, and which ending you are offered — two players rarely see the same story.
